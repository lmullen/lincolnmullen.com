Oxford University Press has just recently released John Fea's [*The Bible Cause: A History of the American Bible Society*](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-bible-cause-9780190253066?cc=us&lang=en&) (2016). The American Bible Society was a central institution of American religious life in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and its significance extended to such topics as evangelism, print culture, benevolence, global missions, and capitalism. This blog will feature a series of reviews of *The Bible Cause* by historians whose work also touches on those topics.

<!--more-->

The symposium included these posts at *Religion in American History*:

- Elesha Coffman, author of [*The Christian Century and the Rise of the Protestant Mainline*](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-christian-century-and-the-rise-of-the-protestant-mainline-9780199938599?cc=us&lang=en&), "[Inside baseball and *The Bible Cause*](https://usreligion.blogspot.com/2016/04/inside-baseball-and-bible-cause.html)"
- Candy Gunther Brown, author of *The Word in the World: Evangelical Writing, Publishing, and Reading in America, 1789–1880* (UNC Press, 2004), "[The language of the ABS](https://usreligion.blogspot.com/2016/04/the-language-of-abs.html)"
- John Fea's response to his reviewers, "[The ABS and the art of history writing](https://usreligion.blogspot.com/2016/04/the-abs-and-art-of-history-writing.html)"

I'd also like to draw readers' attention to [a review of *The Bible Cause* by Paul Harvey](http://religiondispatches.org/theres-no-business-like-the-bible-business-200-years-of-the-abs/) at *Religion Dispatches*. Harvey's thoughtful review responds to the reviews by Coffman and Brown, and is well worth reading alongside them.
