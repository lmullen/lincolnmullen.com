I [use AI](/blog/behind-ahead/) for my work, for which this blog is a hub. Using AI necessarily raises questions of authorship and attribution. Who did the writing, the author or his agent? Is this blog the work of a machine or of my mind? Of course the mind is never unmediated by technology. I hope my readers will not begrudge me the use of the codex and the [text editor](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674417076)[^1] if I call the thoughts posted here my own. But still, having AI write is fundamentally different from using those other assistive technologies for writing, even though I don't know how to articulate why it is different.

So for myself at least, and perhaps for my readers, I find it necessary to draw the line where I will and won't use AI in my work. For the purposes of this website, here is where I draw the line right now.

<!--more-->

- Anything that bears my name, I take responsibility for. If there is an error in fact or judgment, I accept it as mine, just as I would for a work of scholarship aided by a research assistant. But in the same way, I also claim the credit: if the AI helped me produce something useful, it was because I used it skillfully.
- I will not use AI to write prose.[^2] If you read words under my byline, you can be sure that I wrote them.[^3]
- When writing prose, I will use AI in much the same way I would employ a copyeditor. A copyeditor or proofreader has often pointed out my errors, but I have chosen my own corrections.
- I have no compunction about using AI for writing code. This website is [important to me](/blog/working-on-it-1/), and for a long time I had written its every line of code. Now the code is mostly AI-generated and better for it. This blog has only ever half been about the writing. It's always been a technological playground, which I've reinvented whenever I needed to learn something new. I suppose that's what I've been doing here recently.

The norms for writing and AI are emerging, and are already sharply diverging between the disciplines. These are my norms, at least for this website and at least for now.

[^1]: Matthew G. Kirschenbaum, *Track Changes: A Literary History of Word Processing* (The Belknap Press of Harvard University Press, 2016).

[^2]: I make no such promises about poetry.

[^3]: I have always written every post on this site. I briefly used AI to generate the social media posts that link to the blog posts. Take that as a sign of my fundamental disrespect for social media. But now even those social media posts are written by me too. Tellingly, the only other place I use AI for writing is meaningless administrative documents.
