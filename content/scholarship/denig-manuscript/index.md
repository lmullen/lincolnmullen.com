abstract: >-
  The Denig Manuscript Project brings an eighteenth-century, Pennsylvania-made
  manuscript and watercolors to life through a collaborative multimedia digital
  project. High-resolution digital images, updated translations, forensic
  analysis, sound recordings, and contextual scholarship provide enhanced access
  to this extraordinary document of religious life in early America.

  Ludwig Denig (1755–1830) was a shoemaker and apothecary living in colonial and
  revolutionary Pennsylvania. He drew on the devotional and biblical
  spirituality of regional Lutheran and Dutch Reformed churches to create, in
  1784, a leather-bound volume in ink and watercolor that combines a number of
  devotional, theological, and artistic traditions. The manuscript remained in
  private hands until the 1970s, and Winterthur Museum, Garden &amp; Library
  acquired it as a gift in 2020. Winterthur and a team of scholars worked with
  the Roy Rosenzweig Center for History and New Media to build the site.
