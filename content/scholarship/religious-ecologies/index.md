abstract: >-
  The project creates new datasets from historical sources and new ways of
  visualizing them, in order to map America’s religious landscape.

  In the nineteenth century, the U.S. government collected data about churches
  as part of its decennial population census, but when Congress created the
  Census Bureau as a permanent agency in 1902, it authorized it to undertake a
  separate decennial survey of “religious bodies.” Every ten years from 1906 to
  1946, the Census Bureau surveyed congregations, synagogues, and other
  religious groups in the United States.

  While the Census Bureau published summary reports from that data, the forms
  (or schedules) filled out by each congregation have not been widely used.
  Only the schedules from the 1926 census survive, housed at the National
  Archives and Records Administration in Washington, DC. The project has
  digitized 227,733 of those schedules, from 217 denominations in 3,091
  counties, and turns them into data, maps, and visualizations of the early
  twentieth-century American religious landscape.
